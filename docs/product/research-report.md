# Fix the funding model before writing code

The central problem is legal, not technical: the step this business depends on, where clients bid with a UPI ID the operator supplies and the operator approves the mandate from their own bank account, is exactly what SEBI's circulars and FAQs call a "third party linked bank account UPI ID". Those documents say such applications are "not considered valid for allocation" and that registrars must technically reject them. The fixed-fee ("Kostak") split also fits the Benami Act's definition of property held by one person and paid for by another, which carries 1-7 years' rigorous imprisonment. It also repeats the pattern behind SEBI's 2003-05 IPO-scam orders, which the Supreme Court upheld in 2016 and which reached financiers as well as account holders. Grey-market deals in unallotted shares sit outside the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act's spot-delivery exemption and are widely described as unenforceable. Automating the current model would make each of these problems bigger and leave a permanent record of them. A product built on a different model is feasible and cheap: each client funds, bids and approves from their own account, and the software coordinates, reminds, tracks and settles a service fee. A Go + PostgreSQL 18 backend on one Mumbai VM, two Expo SDK 58 apps, an Astro site and a Vite admin panel run for roughly ₹1,500 a month at 100 users, plus about ₹150 for WhatsApp. The largest recurring cost would be the Claude subscription that powers the agent pipeline. Nothing here is legal advice, and most legal findings come from search-engine summaries of primary texts rather than the texts themselves. The section on evidence below says which.

## SEBI rules invalidate the operator-supplied UPI at the core

**The third-party rule is settled at circular and FAQ level, and has been restated recently.** The 2018 SEBI UPI-phase circular, as reproduced by the Calcutta Stock Exchange, says in clause 10.2 that "applications made using third party bank account or using third party linked bank account UPI ID are liable for rejection" and that the "RTA shall undertake technical rejection of all applications" made this way ([CSE notice](https://www.cse-india.com/upload/upload/November_012018.pdf)). SEBI's 2019 FAQ goes further: applications "using third party UPI id or by any category of investors using a third party bank account will not be considered valid for allocation" ([SEBI FAQ 2019](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/jul-2019/1564465350843_p.pdf)). SEBI's **May 2025 ICDR FAQ repeats the rule** ([SEBI FAQ May 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/faqfiles/may-2025/1747290561386.pdf)), and a **9 February 2026 Master Circular** reportedly consolidates PAN matching between bank and demat records and the registrar's third-party check ([NSDL copy](https://nsdl.co.in/downloadables/pdf/05_SEBI_Circular_dated_09_February_2026.pdf)). The check compares the PAN behind the UPI-linked bank account with the applicant's PAN ([Union Bank SEBI guidelines](https://www.unionbankofindia.co.in/pdf/New%20SEBI%20Guidlines-7.pdf)). The only exception covers joint holders of the same bank account. An operator-owned account in which clients are not holders does not qualify. Brokers say the same: using "a spouse's or friend's UPI ID breaks the third-party rule and the bid is thrown out" ([IPOMarket](https://www.ipomarket.in/news/what-to-do-if-your-ipo-application-is); [Kotak Securities](https://kotaksecurities.com/support/upi-rejections-ipo-applications)). Market practitioners acknowledge that some third-party bids still get allotted when banks fail to return PAN data ([Chittorgarh FAQ](https://chittorgarh.com/faq_pg/why-do-a-few-3rd-party-upi-ipo-applications-get-an-allotment/3995)). That gap is a validation failure, not a lawful route. An allotment obtained through it was obtained against an express condition in SEBI's rules and the offer document, which adds a misrepresentation angle to the rejection risk. Even IPO Premium, the benchmark app, says in its own terms that third-party bank or UPI applications are prohibited ([IPO Premium terms](https://www.ipopremium.in/terms)).

**The IPO-scam precedent covers financiers, not just the people whose names are used.** In the 2003-05 demat scam, Karvy's DP opened accounts for **6,315 benamis of Roopalben Panchal**. In 2011 Panchal and five others were ordered to disgorge **₹36.09 crore** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/ipo-scam-sebi-slaps-rs-36-crore-penalty-on-six-111022600023_1.html)). SEBI separately pursued Dushyant Dalal, whose show-cause notice alleged he "provided finance to enable them to apply for shares in the retail category of 10 IPOs", and reported a **₹14 crore penalty on two IPO-scam financiers** in June 2011 ([Legitquest](https://www.legitquest.com/case/in-re-ipo-irregularities-dealing-by-mr-dushyant-natwarlal-dalal-and-mrs-puloma-dushyant-dalal/11A2A1); [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/sebi-imposes-rs14-crore-penalty-on-two-ipo-scam-financiers/17006.html)). In July 2016 the Supreme Court restored SEBI's disgorgement orders, holding that the demat account holders "were not genuine and either they were benami or fictitious" ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/sc-rules-in-favour-of-sebi-in-ipo-scam-case-116071300865_1.html)). The research has two important qualifications. That scam mostly involved fictitious or mass-opened accounts, not 50 real, consenting people. And the research found **no 2023-2026 SEBI order aimed squarely at an individual funding consenting retail applicants for a profit share**. The current concern is real, though: in January 2024 SEBI's chair said "many mule accounts are used to bid" in IPOs ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/these-two-ipo-market-practices-have-come-under-sebi-scanner-414138-2024-01-19)). In September 2026 NSE named Telegram "account handling" operators and called such activities "illegal" ([NSE press release](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com//web/pressrelease/2026-09/PR_cc_08092026_0_20260908124751.pdf)).

**The Benami Act fits the fixed-fee model closely.** Section 2(9) defines a benami transaction as property "held by" one person but "provided or paid for by another". The exceptions require a close-family or HUF relationship and payment from known sources. Section 53 carries **1-7 years' rigorous imprisonment and a fine up to 25% of fair market value** ([Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/benami-transaction-act); [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/offences-and-penalties-under-the-benami-transactions-act/)). In the fixed-fee model the operator puts up the money, takes the entire gain or loss at the NSE listing price, and pays the registered holder a flat fee. That is the closest possible match to "held by A, paid for by B, for B's benefit". The 20-40%-of-gain model looks more like a loan plus a profit share. That makes the Benami case weaker, but it raises **state money-lending law** instead. The Gujarat Money-Lenders Act 2011, for example, bars "business of money-lending except registration" (s.5) and makes unregistered lending an offence (s.42). Regulated NBFCs are outside these laws ([India Code](https://indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/3222?locale=en); [Moneylife on NBFC carve-out](https://moneylife.in/article/moneylending-laws-not-applicable-to-nbfcs-holds-gujarat-high-court/16205.html)). Tax practitioners point to the same split from the other direction. Listing gains are taxed at **20% STCG** in the PAN holder's hands ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/short-term-capital-gain-on-shares)). When the operator funds the bid and keeps the gain, the economic owner and the legal owner differ, and a TaxGuru piece describes benami IPO applications as carrying a "criminal overtone" ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/opening-demat-account-benami-making-large-ipo-application-involves-criminal-overtone.html)).

**Grey-market contracts are not illegal on their face, but they are unenforceable and unprotected.** No statute mentions the "grey market". SCRA s.18 exempts only "spot delivery contracts", defined in s.2(i) as delivery and payment on the same or next day ([SEBI copy of SCRA](https://www.sebi.gov.in/acts/contractact.pdf)). SEBI adjudication orders treat off-market contracts "simply for future performance" as breaching s.13, with penalties under s.23H ([SEBI AO Nov 2024](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/nov-2024/1732189803210_1.pdf)). A student law-journal analysis argues that Kostak and Subject-to-Sauda deals are wagering contracts, void under s.30 of the Contract Act ([CNLU CARCIL](https://carcil.cnlu.ac.in/?p=385)). Commentators disagree on whether this makes them "not illegal, but not authorised" ([Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/ipo-gmp-meaning-grey-market-premium)) or "not legal and disapproved" ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/stock-market-guide/ipo/what-is-grey-market)). They agree that SEBI has not acted against Kostak trading itself, and that there is **no recourse through SEBI or the exchanges if a counterparty defaults** ([IPOMarket, Oct 2026](https://www.ipomarket.in/news/is-ipo-grey-market-legal-india-sebi-rules-risks)). In practice, neither the operator nor a client could reliably enforce the split in court. Since January 2025 SEBI has floated a regulated **"when-listed" platform** for trading between allotment and listing ([Business Standard, Jan 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/ipo/sebi-to-start-pre-listing-trading-in-ipo-to-curb-grey-market-activity-buch-125012100843_1.html); [Business Standard, Aug 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/sebi-may-introduce-regulated-platform-for-pre-ipo-firms-tuhin-kanta-pandey-125082100581_1.html)). It has not launched. If it does, it would compete with grey-market price discovery; it would not legitimise third-party funding.

**What a lawful version looks like.** The rules point to one shape. Each client applies with their **own PAN, demat account and bank/UPI**, using money from their own known sources. Any loan is documented, at arm's length, and made by a registered money-lender, an NBFC, or a bank, which may now lend up to **₹25 lakh per individual for IPOs** following RBI's October 2025 change ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/rbi-to-raise-ipo-financing-and-loans-against-shares-limits-125100101020_1.html)). There is no pre-agreed sale of the allotment and no shared login credentials. The operator's legitimate role then becomes a coordination service paid by a fee. That fee is business income at slab rates for the operator. GST registration applies above roughly ₹20 lakh of turnover ([PhonePe Business](https://business.phonepe.com/articles/payment-gateway-without-gst-do-you-need-registration-to-accept-payments)). If the operator recommends specific IPOs, there is a weaker but real unregistered-advisory question. The research found **no law-firm note analysing this exact scenario**. A securities lawyer and a CA should review the redesigned model before launch.

**How much of this rests on primary texts.** Direct fetches to sebi.gov.in, npci.org.in and most market sites failed during the research. Almost every legal finding therefore comes from **search-engine summaries** of the cited documents. That includes the third-party circulars and FAQs, the Benami and SCRA provisions, the scam orders, the RBI change and the money-lending Act. The exact wording of the February 2026 Master Circular, any 2025-26 offer document, the PFUTP regulation numbers and the s.16 SCRA notification were not read. The legal conclusions that rest on inference, not on any source, are: how Benami applies to consenting real holders, how money-lending law applies to the profit-share model, and the "fraudulent device" reading of supplying one's own UPI ID.

## Payments, tax, privacy and app stores narrow the product further

**UPI no longer lets an individual request money.** NPCI switched off **P2P collect requests from 1 October 2025**; merchant collect continues ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/npci-to-end-upi-p2p-collect-requests-from-october-1-to-reduce-fraud)). The research found no report of a deferral. Settlement therefore has to work through `upi://pay` intent links or QR codes pointing to the payee's VPA, pre-filled with the amount and a reference. Person-to-person transfers stay free. A merchant VPA would allow collect requests and dynamic QR. But from **15 October 2026, merchant (P2M) UPI payments above ₹2,000 carry 0.4% MDR capped at ₹300**, with small-merchant exemptions whose threshold the research could not find ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/09/govt-sets-04-fee-on-upi-merchant-payments-above-rs-2000-caps-charge-at-rs-300)). A merchant VPA also creates a visible business-receipts trail. Some banks cap payments made from a QR image uploaded from the gallery at ₹2,000 ([Kotak](https://www.kotak.com/en/help-center/bill-payment-and-recharge/scan-and-pay/what-are-the-transaction-limits.html)), so clients should scan with the camera or pay to the VPA directly. Payment gateways are a weak dependency. Razorpay onboards unregistered individuals on a personal PAN, but it mandates third-party validation for securities-related businesses ([Razorpay BFSI](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/solutions/bfsi.md)). Typical Indian payment-aggregator prohibited lists include unregulated investment schemes and person-to-person money transfers ([Zoho Payments](https://www.zoho.com/in/payments/prohibited-restricted-businesses.html)). Payout APIs themselves are cheap, at **₹6-15 per UPI/IMPS transfer** on Cashfree, with virtual-account collections from ₹20 ([Cashfree Payouts](https://www.cashfree.com/en/payouts/)). The hard part is getting onboarded honestly. Account Aggregator data is effectively closed: only entities regulated by RBI, SEBI, IRDAI or PFRDA can act as data users ([Setu](https://docs.setu.co/data/account-aggregator/licenses-and-go-live/licenses)). That leaves reconciliation through imported bank statements, parsing the operator's own bank-alert emails, and client-entered UTR numbers matched on amount, date and payer.

**Data protection already applies, and the full DPDP regime starts in May 2027.** The DPDP Rules were notified on 13/14 November 2025. Consent-manager rules apply from about now (November 2026). Notice, consent, security, breach-reporting and erasure duties apply from about **13 May 2027** ([AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect/)). The research found no small-entity exemption. Penalties reach **₹250 crore** for failing to keep reasonable security safeguards ([BDO](https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/news/2025/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-%E2%80%94-check-provisions,-implementation-plan,-penalties)). Until May 2027 the SPDI Rules 2011 continue to apply ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1713500/regulatory-update-notification-of-the-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025)). CERT-In's 2022 Directions separately require **incident reports within 6 hours** and **180 days of logs kept in India** ([Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/en/insights/blogs/digilinks/2022/may/india-new-and-onerous-cyber-security-framework-and-breach-reporting-obligations)). One open question is whether these directions bind an individual operator rather than a company. An app holding PAN numbers, demat IDs, bank identifiers and money balances should therefore do five things: encrypt those fields, mask what it displays, record per-channel consent with timestamps, keep logs in Mumbai, and support in-app account deletion.

**Apple's rules effectively rule out a public iOS listing, and Android has narrow private routes.** App Review Guideline 3.2.1(viii) says apps "used for financial trading, investing, or money management should be submitted by the financial institution performing such services" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Apple itself steers family-and-friends apps to Ad Hoc distribution, and TestFlight builds must be "intended for public distribution" and expire after 90 days. With **iOS at about 5% of Indian mobile traffic** ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/india)), a PWA or TestFlight is a reasonable fallback. On Android, Play's **internal testing track holds up to 100 testers** with no 12-tester, 14-day gate ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334)). New personal Play accounts need that 14-day closed test before going to production; organisation accounts with a D-U-N-S number are exempt ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en)). Every app must complete the financial-features declaration, and new apps must target **API 36 from 31 August 2026** ([Play target API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11926878?hl=en-419)). SMS-reading permissions are limited to default handlers and narrow exceptions, and developers report rejections ([Play SMS policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820?hl=en)). Directly sideloaded APKs still work in India. However, Google's developer-verification requirement took effect on **30 September 2026** in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand and is planned to go global in 2027 ([Google](https://support.google.com/android-developer-console/answer/16561738?hl=en)). Any sideloaded operator APK should therefore be signed under a verified identity before then.

## No official API exists for IPO data, allotment or bulk apply

**IPO Premium's bulk apply almost certainly depends on a registered intermediary, and that intermediary is not public.** The app is published by Jaynath Infotech Pvt Ltd of Savarkundla, Gujarat. It reports 1M+ Play downloads at about 4.8 stars ([Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ipopremium.app&hl=en_IN)) and advertises "Apply with UPI from all your demat accounts" and "allotment checks for every account in one go" ([ipopremium.in](https://www.ipopremium.in/login)). Its FAQ refers to an exchange-generated application number appearing on the mandate ([FAQ](https://www.ipopremium.in/faq)), which implies a trading or syndicate member is entering the bids. Its terms say it is not a SEBI-registered broker ([Terms](https://www.ipopremium.in/terms)). The research could not identify the member behind it. The only documented programmatic routes are **Upstox's beta `POST /ipos/orders`**, which works only for the authenticated client's own account ([Upstox docs](https://upstox.com/developer/api-documentation/apply-ipo/)), and **NSE e-IPO API and bulk upload, which only registered members can use** ([NSE e-IPO FAQs](https://www.nseindia.com/static/trade/e-ipo-faqs)). Kite Connect has no IPO API ([kite.trade forum](https://kite.trade/forum/discussion/13664/ipo-subscribe-api)). No broker offers a white-label IPO API to fintechs publicly. Bulk apply in version 1 should therefore be a per-client checklist that deep-links into each client's own broker, tracks whether each mandate was approved, and calls the Upstox API on the client's own OAuth token where the client uses Upstox. Full bulk submission requires a partnership with a SEBI-registered broker, for example as an Authorised Person or technology partner. Duplicates do not help. Applying from several apps produces several entries under one PAN, and they are all rejected ([IPOMarket](https://www.ipomarket.in/news/multiple-ipo-apps-pan-rule-allotment-chances); [Alice Blue](https://aliceblueonline.com/blog/ipo-multiple-applications-one-pan)).

**Calendar and subscription data have no clean free feed.** NSE's Terms of Use prohibit "systematic or automated data collection activities (including scraping…)" ([NSE ToU](https://www.nseindia.com/nse-terms-of-use)). Its JSON endpoints need a cookie handshake and are not a product dependency anyone should rely on. The cleanest version-1 route is to curate data by hand from offer documents and NSE/BSE circulars, which publish the symbol and ISIN before listing ([circular mirror](https://rhnvrm.github.io/stock-market-circulars/tags/new-listing/)). That can be supplemented by **ipoalerts.in**, which has a free API tier of 25 requests a day, or 750 a month ([ipoalerts](https://ipoalerts.in/ipo-data-api)). Apify actors at **$3-50 per 1,000 records** are scrapers themselves, so they move the terms-of-use risk to someone else rather than removing it ([Apify](https://apify.com/data_daemon/india-ipo-tracker)). GMP has no licensed source at all. Studies (none peer-reviewed) find it is right on direction about 70-80% of the time and weaker for SME issues ([ipomarket GMP tracker](https://www.ipomarket.in/gmp-accuracy)). Average 2025 listing gains of about 10% compare with about 30% in 2024 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/ipo/gmps-vs-ipo-returns-what-2025-s-ipo-numbers-say-about-gmps-125122200065_1.html)). Leave GMP out, or link out to the publishers with an "unofficial" label.

**Allotment status can be checked one record at a time, not through any official bulk channel.** The registrars (KFintech and MUFG Intime handle over 80% of issues, Bigshare, Cameo and Skyline the rest) and BSE expose lookup pages by PAN, application number or DP/client ID. There is no API ([ipomarket registrar guide](https://www.ipomarket.in/news/how-to-check-ipo-allotment-status-all-registrars)). Sources disagree on captchas. Guides say most portals show one. A 2026 open-source Telegram bot says only Cameo uses an image captcha, while MUFG uses an encrypted page token and KFintech, Bigshare and others expose JSON calls. That bot polls every 30 minutes on allotment day ([AbGisHere bot](https://github.com/AbGisHere/IPO-Allotment-Status-Bot)). Commercial apps clearly automate these checks. A defensible version 1 does three things. It deep-links each account to the right registrar page so the user solves any captcha. It ingests the **SEBI-mandated bank unblock SMS** and the depository credit alerts on T+2 ([SEBI 2021 UPI circular via MSEI](https://www.msei.in/SX-Content/Company-Circulars/2021/June/SEBI-02-Jun-2021-STREAMLINING-THE-PROCESS-OF-IPOS-WITH-UPI-IN-ASBA-AND-REDRESSAL-OF-INVESTOR-GRIEVANCES..pdf)), forwarded by the user. And at most it runs low-rate, user-triggered checks against captcha-free endpoints after a legal review. It never solves captchas programmatically. Timing follows T+3, mandatory since December 2023: bids and mandates close at 5 pm on day T, allotment on T+1, unblock and share credit on T+2, listing at 10 am on T+3 ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/reduction-timeline-listing-shares-public-issue-t-3-days.html); [Rupeezy](https://support.rupeezy.in/support/solutions/articles/21000005340-how-long-does-it-take-for-ipos-to-list-on-the-exchange-)). That gives the scheduler fixed anchors for reminders.

**The "NSE listing price" is the pre-open equilibrium price, and showing it to many users live counts as licensed redistribution.** The listing-day special pre-open runs 9:00-10:00, with order entry closing at a random time around 9:45. Its equilibrium price is common to NSE and BSE and becomes the open ([NSE FAQ](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/FAQs%20on%20Special%20Pre-Open%20Session.pdf)). A May 2026 SEBI consultation proposed changing the dummy price bands, and its outcome was not confirmed ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/sebi-proposes-tighter-rules-for-options-strike-management/article/sebi-moves-to-end-dummy-price-band-distortions-in-ipo-trading-proposes-dynamic-price-band-expansion/80541.html)). Redistributing real-time NSE data needs an NSE Data & Analytics licence ([NSE non-display policy](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/Non_Display_Policy.pdf)). For internal settlement, the operator can read the price from their own broker data API (Kite Connect costs **₹500 a month**, Upstox data is free) and record it ([Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/kite-api/articles/what-are-the-charges-for-kite-apis)). Whether broker terms allow showing that price to clients was not confirmed.

## A Go monolith on one Mumbai VM runs for about ₹1,500 a month

**Go is the best-supported non-Node backend choice for both the product and the user's career.** Zerodha writes "performance-critical, high throughput services" in Go and its back-office in Python ([Zerodha Tech](https://zerodha.tech/blog/hello-world/)). Secondary sources place Go in newer services at Razorpay and PhonePe ([hirestepx](https://hirestepx.com/blog/best-fintech-companies-india-2026)). Go job listings in India are fewer than Python's but pay a premium, though salary figures conflict between sources ([upGrad](https://www.upgrad.com/blog/highest-paying-programming-languages-in-india/)). Raw throughput does not decide anything at 100 users. What matters is footprint and agent feedback. Go 1.26 made the Green Tea garbage collector the default, cutting GC overhead by 10-40% ([InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4131097/go-1-26-unleashes-performance-boosting-green-tea-gc.html)), and a Go API, worker and scheduler fit together in a 512 MB container. Benchmarks of AI coding agents vary by model and scaffold, and none shows Go losing ([SWE-Bench ProMax](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.09802)). The recommended pieces are:

- the standard library router or chi;
- Huma v2 or oapi-codegen for OpenAPI 3.1, with generated TypeScript clients;
- pgx v5.13 with sqlc and goose;
- **River** for Postgres-backed jobs, which are enqueued inside the same transaction as the ledger write ([River](https://github.com/riverqueue/river); [pgx](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/jackc/pgx/v5)).

The ledger should be an append-only double-entry ledger in integer paise, in the style of pgledger ([pgledger](https://www.pgrs.net/2025/06/17/double-entry-ledgers-missing-primitive-in-modern-software/)). Deal terms should carry effective dates, settlements should carry idempotency keys, and the application, allotment and settlement steps should run as explicit state machines. **PostgreSQL 18** adds `uuidv7()` and `OLD/NEW` in `RETURNING`, which suits audit trails ([PostgreSQL 18](https://www.postgresql.org/about/news/postgresql-18-released-3142/)). Python/FastAPI is the reasonable alternative, or a small sidecar if scraping grows. Rust is overkill for this product.

**Host in Mumbai, and keep the database on the same VM until uptime needs justify more.** Mumbai saves roughly **50-90 ms per round trip** compared with Singapore for most Indian users ([Techplained](https://www.techplained.com/india-cloud-latency)). Render has no India region, Railway users are still asking for one, and Hetzner raised prices twice in 2026 ([Render](https://render.com/articles/platforms-with-a-real-free-tier-for-developers-in-2026); [Northflank](https://northflank.com/blog/hetzner-cloud-server-price-increases)). The research compared three set-ups. The simplest is a **Lightsail Mumbai 2 GB instance at $12 a month** with public IPv4 included ([Lightsail](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lightsail/latest/userguide/amazon-lightsail-bundles.html)), running the API, worker and PostgreSQL 18, deployed with Kamal, with WAL-G backups to Cloudflare R2. R2 includes 10 GB free and charges no egress ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)). A serverless option pairs Cloud Run in asia-south1, which gets the full free tier ([Cloud Run pricing](https://cloud.google.com/run/pricing)), or Fly.io's `bom` region at **$2.19 a month per machine** ([Fly.io](https://fly.io/pricing-update/)), with **Supabase Pro at $25** in ap-south-1 ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing.md)). Fly's Mumbai region had several incidents in 2026, and Supabase's free tier pauses after 7 idle days. An AWS-native build with Fargate, an ALB and RDS costs an estimated $60-85 a month; those totals are the research's own arithmetic and partly unverified. Cloudflare's free plan handles DNS, WAF and Tunnel, which can hide the admin panel behind Access. KMS envelope encryption of PAN and bank fields costs about **$1 per key per month** ([AWS KMS](https://aws.amazon.com/kms/pricing/)). Sentry, Grafana Cloud and PostHog free tiers cover observability ([costbench](https://costbench.com/software/observability/grafana-cloud/free-plan/)). PostHog hosts only in the US or EU, so personal data should be scrubbed before it reaches PostHog. CERT-In logs should go to India-resident storage.

**On the frontend, use Expo SDK 58 for two separate apps, Astro for the marketing site, and a Vite SPA for the admin panel.** Expo SDK 58 shipped on 15 October 2026 on React Native 0.88. It turns R8 on by default for release builds, makes native tabs stable, and recommends development builds over Expo Go ([Expo SDK 58](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58); [RN 0.88](https://reactnative.dev/blog/2026/10/19/react-native-0.88)). Hermes V1 has been the default engine since React Native 0.84 ([RN 0.84](https://reactnative.dev/blog/2026/02/11/react-native-0.84)). Ship **separate operator and client apps** that share `ui`, `api-client` and `tokens` packages in a pnpm + Turborepo monorepo ([Expo monorepos](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/monorepos/)). That way any sensitive operator permission, such as a notification listener for bank alerts in a local Expo module, never ships to clients. Other recommended pieces:

- styling: Uniwind or HeroUI Native rather than the NativeWind v5 release candidate, which a vendor benchmark ranks slowest ([Uniwind](https://uniwind.dev/vs-nativewind); [HeroUI Native](https://heroui.com/en/docs/native/releases));
- data and forms: TanStack Query persisted to MMKV v4, with React Hook Form and Zod;
- tooling: TypeScript 7, the native Go compiler, which is about 8-12 times faster ([howtodoinjava](https://howtodoinjava.com/typescript/typescript-7-native-compiler/));
- marketing site: Astro on Cloudflare Pages, which allows commercial use on the free tier (Vercel Hobby does not) ([flaviocopes](https://flaviocopes.com/hosting-free-tiers/));
- admin panel: React 19, TanStack Router/Table and shadcn/ui, or TanStack Start 1.0 if server-side rendering is wanted;
- testing: Maestro for end-to-end mobile tests (free locally, with a built-in MCP server) ([Maestro MCP](https://maestro.dev/mcp)).

The EAS free tier's 1K update MAUs covers 100 users, and Starter costs $19 a month ([Expo pricing](https://expo.dev/pricing)).

**Notifications need several channels, because push alone is unreliable on Indian phones.** Since 1 July 2025 Meta has charged per WhatsApp template message. India rates are about **₹0.115 for utility and authentication messages** and ₹0.8631 for marketing. From **1 October 2026, service replies are charged too**, after 1,000 free a month ([MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026); [BW Businessworld](https://www.businessworld.in/article/whatsapp-business-charges-rise-as-meta-bills-service-messages-626463)). About 1,000 utility reminders a month cost roughly ₹136 including GST on the direct Cloud API. A BSP's ₹1,500-3,500 monthly platform fee would cost 10 to 30 times more than the messages themselves. Financial services are a restricted Commerce Policy category ([Rasayel](https://docs.rasayel.io/whatsapp-commerce-policy)). Allotment and payment-due notices read as utility messages, but "apply for IPO X" will probably be classed as marketing. Unofficial libraries such as Baileys put the operator's number at risk of a permanent ban ([checkleaked](https://whatsapp.checkleaked.cc/blog/best-open-source-whatsapp-libraries)). Expo push wraps FCM and is free. One vendor estimates that **about 40% of pushes are not visible on Chinese-OEM phones** because of battery optimisation ([Pushwoosh](https://www.pushwoosh.com/blog/why-are-your-android-push-campaigns-not-delivered/)). Deadline alerts should therefore escalate in steps: an in-app inbox as the source of truth, then push, then local scheduled reminders, then a WhatsApp utility template, then a manual call list. Notifee was archived in April 2026 ([dev.to](https://dev.to/marco_crupi/notifee-is-archived-heres-a-maintained-new-architecture-drop-in-replacement-3ib5)). For login with about 100 invited users, use:

- an invite QR code or link carrying a single-use token;
- a one-time **WhatsApp authentication OTP** (about ₹0.115, with one-tap autofill on Android) ([8x8](https://developer.8x8.com/connect/docs/guide-whatsapp-one-tap-or-zero-tap-authentication)), or a pre-registered SMS OTP route at ₹0.11-0.25 per message ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/?p=16670));
- a Keystore-bound session unlocked by biometrics, with passkeys as an optional upgrade.

Firebase phone auth needs the Blaze plan, and sources disagree on its India rate: about $0.01 per SMS according to one source, $0.06-0.07 according to others ([Logto](https://blog.logto.io/firebase-authentication-pricing); [metacto](https://www.metacto.com/blogs/the-complete-guide-to-firebase-auth-costs-setup-integration-and-maintenance)). Running your own DLT SMS registration costs about ₹6,000 upfront, and every URL in a message must be whitelisted ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/trai-pushes-coordinated-crackdown-on-spam-and-cyber-fraud-1600-series-rollout-url-whitelisting-and-blacklisting-of-entities-on-agenda-82910.htm)). ## Claude Code enforces quality through hooks and tests, not specs

**Spec Kit 1.4 and Claude Code 2.1.323 together cover the whole pipeline, but a deterministic gate has to define "done".** Spec Kit reached **v1.4.0 on 10 November 2026** and now uses hyphenated commands (`/speckit-specify`, `-plan`, `-tasks`, `-implement`, and a new `-converge`). It also offers first-party `bug` and `assess` extensions ([PyPI specify-cli](https://pypi.org/project/specify-cli/)). It shipped eleven patch releases in September alone, plus supply-chain pinning and the ability to render its commands as Claude Code skills ([Spec Kit Sept 2026 newsletter](https://github.com/github/spec-kit/blob/main/newsletters/2026-September.md)). Thoughtworks' Birgitta Böckeler calls it "the most customizable but heaviest" of the spec-driven tools. She warns about spec-implementation drift and reports a bug fix that ballooned into 16 acceptance criteria in Kiro ([Martin Fowler](https://www.martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/sdd-3-tools.html)). The research found no independent benchmark of spec-driven development against a no-spec baseline. The workable split is: Spec Kit for features that cross the API and the apps; the `bug` extension, or "write a failing test, then fix", for defects; and plan mode or a `SPEC.md` interview for small tasks, which is Claude Code's own recommendation ([best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). Pin the Spec Kit version, given how often it releases.

**Anthropic's guidance favours a small agent roster, fresh-context review, and hooks.** Claude Code subagents take per-agent `model`, `effort`, `tools`, `hooks`, `mcpServers` and `isolation: worktree` settings. Nesting is limited to 3 levels and concurrency to 20 agents. **Plugin subagents silently ignore hooks and MCP servers**, so enforcement has to live in project agents ([Subagents docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)). The docs say plainly that "handing the same task to a subagent doesn't make the answer any better. A fresh reviewer does help". They also recommend putting must-always rules in hooks, deny rules or the sandbox rather than in CLAUDE.md, which should stay under about 200 lines ([best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). A **Stop hook that runs the tests and the type check, and exits with code 2 on failure**, keeps the agent working until the checks pass ([Hooks docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)). A sensible roster is: a spec writer and planner on Opus; per-stack implementers in worktrees (Go, React Native, web) scoped by `.claude/rules/` paths; a test writer; a read-only reviewer that checks against the spec; a security reviewer; and QA agents driving Maestro and Playwright through MCP. The CI gates are golangci-lint, `go test -race`, Biome or oxlint, `tsc`, OpenAPI diff, migration lint, and Maestro on an Android emulator. AI review stays advisory. Managed Code Review is available **only on Team and Enterprise plans** ([Code Review docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review)). A solo developer on Max should use local `/code-review` plus `anthropics/claude-code-action@v1` with the code-review plugin, authenticated by a subscription OAuth token or an API key ([GitHub Actions docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/github-actions)). Third-party AI reviewers have only vendor-run benchmarks that contradict each other, and Sentry's Seer code review is being removed on 8 December 2026 ([Sentry docs](https://docs.sentry.io/product/issues/issue-details/sentry-ai)).

**Useful official MCP servers exist, but payment and broker tools should stay out of unattended runs.** Expo's hosted MCP server exposes build, update and TestFlight tools but **requires a paid EAS plan** ([Expo MCP](https://docs.expo.dev/eas/ai/mcp)). Maestro's MCP server runs from its CLI. Figma's write-to-canvas tool is in beta. Razorpay and Zerodha Kite both publish official MCP servers ([Razorpay MCP](https://razorpay.com/docs/mcp-server/remote?preferred-country=SG); [Kite MCP](https://github.com/zerodha/kite-mcp-server)). Prompt injection through tool outputs is the main documented risk ([Doppler](https://www.doppler.com/guides/mcp-server-security-risks-attack-scenarios/prompt-injection-triggers)). Scope MCP servers to specific agents, use test keys only, and block payout tools with deny rules. A daily Sentry-triage routine on Haiku can open issues. When a human labels an issue `agent-fix`, claude-code-action can take it to a PR with `--max-turns` and a budget cap, and a human still merges. Published caps on cloud Routines (scheduled cloud runs) vary between sources (Pro 5 a day, Max 15) and should be checked in the docs before relying on them ([Better Stack](https://betterstack.com/community/guides/ai/claude-code-routines/)).

## Conclusion

Today, the manual WhatsApp-and-spreadsheet process is the only thing keeping this business small, and therefore less visible. A good product would remove that limit and at the same time create a detailed record of operator-funded, third-party UPI applications across dozens of PANs, with a profit split tied to allotment. That is the same evidence SEBI used in the scam-era orders. The engineering is the easy part. Everything above shows a modern, cheap and agent-buildable stack. Before the first commit, the user has to decide which business the software will run. The version that survives the rules is a coordination and reminder service: clients fund and approve from their own accounts, the operator charges a disclosed service fee, and the ledger records who funded each bid. That version also makes a stronger portfolio piece, because it touches real fintech problems (an idempotent ledger, consent records under DPDP, multi-channel deadline delivery, broker-API integration) and none of the regulatory problems.

Two external developments could change this picture. If SEBI launches its regulated when-listed platform, that price would offer a legitimate signal to build on and would weaken the grey market these splits depend on. And a tie-up with a SEBI-registered broker, the route IPO Premium appears to use without saying so, is the only path to true bulk apply. The sensible order is to get legal and CA sign-off on the redesigned model, ship the coordination app to about 100 known Android users through Play internal testing, and pursue a broker partnership only once the model is clean.
